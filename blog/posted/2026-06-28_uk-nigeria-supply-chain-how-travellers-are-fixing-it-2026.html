Why the UK Nigeria Supply Chain Has Always Been Broken

Courier companies price long-haul routes like Nigeria based on fuel, handling, customs processing, and — most importantly — the assumption that you have no alternative. For parcels under 5kg, the economics are particularly punishing. You are effectively subsidising a freight infrastructure built for large commercial shipments, even when all you want to send is a birthday dress, a bag of medicine, or stock for a small shop in Abuja.

Customs clearance adds another layer of uncertainty. Parcels travelling through conventional freight channels are flagged, delayed, and sometimes returned with no explanation. For senders in the UK, that means paying again, waiting again, and explaining to someone on the other end why their parcel has disappeared into a bureaucratic void. The average wait time for a standard courier package from the UK to Nigeria is 10 to 21 working days — and that is on the optimistic end.

Small businesses sourcing or shipping goods between the two countries feel this most acutely. Whether you are importing Nigerian food products into the UK or sending British-made goods out to customers in Lagos, Port Harcourt, or Kano, the cost and unpredictability of traditional logistics eats directly into your margins and your reputation.
