Why Standard Couriers Keep Letting the Diaspora Down

Anyone who has tried to send a parcel from the UK to Nigeria through a traditional courier service knows the frustration well. Declared transit times rarely account for customs delays at Murtala Muhammed International Airport, unexpected public holidays, or the notorious last-mile gap between the courier depot and your family's actual address in Enugu, Abuja, or Port Harcourt. A service quoting ten to fourteen business days often means three weeks in reality.

For time-sensitive moments — birthdays, naming ceremonies, graduations, or a mother who simply needs her medication refilled — those extra days are not an inconvenience. They are a missed connection. The parcel arrives after the moment has passed, and no amount of tracking updates can restore what was lost.

The deeper issue is that standard logistics was never designed with the Nigerian diaspora in mind. It was built for bulk commercial shipments, not for the deeply personal, relationship-driven way that British-Nigerians keep their families close across thousands of miles.
